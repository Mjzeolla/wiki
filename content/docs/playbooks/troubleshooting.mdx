Troubleshooting playbooks begin with an observable symptom rather than a presumed cause. Their goal
is to narrow the failing layer safely and leave enough evidence for recovery or escalation.

## Diagnostic shape

1. Define the exact symptom, scope, start time, and last known-good state.
2. Check recent deployments, configuration, dependency, traffic, and infrastructure changes.
3. Gather logs, metrics, traces, status, and representative failed requests without destroying the
   original evidence.
4. Divide the request path into layers and use one discriminating check to identify the failing side.
5. Form a falsifiable hypothesis and change one variable at a time.
6. Mitigate only after understanding its risk, blast radius, and reversal path.
7. Verify user-visible recovery and record the root cause or remaining uncertainty.

Each symptom page should include expected healthy signals, commands that are safe by default, common
cause classes, stop conditions, and escalation evidence. Separate diagnosis from destructive repair.
Restarting, clearing caches, increasing limits, or deleting state may hide the symptom without
identifying the cause.

Link the final diagnosis to the knowledge page explaining the underlying behavior and to a dedicated
recovery playbook when remediation is repeatable.
