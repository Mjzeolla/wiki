Operate OpenSearch around four constraints: shard availability, resource headroom, recoverability,
and workload latency. Cluster health color is an allocation signal, not a complete service-level
indicator.

## Health semantics

The [Cluster Health API](https://docs.opensearch.org/latest/api-reference/cluster-api/cluster-health/)
reports allocation state at cluster, index, or shard level:

- **Green:** every primary and replica shard is allocated.
- **Yellow:** all primaries are allocated, but at least one replica is not. Reads and writes can work,
  but redundancy is reduced.
- **Red:** at least one primary shard is unallocated, so some data is unavailable.

Monitor search and indexing latency, error and rejection rates, JVM heap and garbage collection,
CPU, disk usage and watermarks, shard count and size, pending cluster tasks, recovery traffic, and
snapshot outcomes. Alert on user-visible symptoms and exhausted safety margins, not health color
alone.

## Capacity and shard movement

Keep capacity for a node or failure-domain loss and the recovery traffic it triggers. Disk watermarks
can prevent allocation or move shards away from full nodes. Before adding nodes, determine whether
the constraint is storage, heap, CPU, network, query concurrency, write throughput, or an inefficient
mapping/query; horizontal capacity does not repair every bottleneck.

## Backups and recovery

Snapshots back up indexes and cluster metadata to a repository and are incremental across successful
snapshots. A snapshot is not a perfectly simultaneous image of every shard. Automate snapshots,
monitor failures, define retention, protect the repository from the cluster's failure domain, and
regularly restore into an isolated environment to prove recovery time and compatibility.

## Changes and upgrades

Before upgrades or rolling restarts, confirm green health, resolve allocation problems, capture a
tested snapshot, check plugin and client compatibility, and define a rollback or restore plan.
OpenSearch nodes cannot simply be downgraded after an incompatible upgrade. Follow the supported
version path and replace or restart nodes in the documented order while watching recovery and health.

Keep exact commands, access requirements, stop conditions, verification, and rollback steps in
[Operations Playbooks](/docs/playbooks/operations). This page explains why those controls exist.
